Riverside Family Clinic respectfully requests general operating support from the Community Health Access Fund to sustain primary care access for uninsured adults across East County.     Why this and not a chatbot

    Built for grants, not for writing in general.

    It won't invent your statistics

    General chatbots fill gaps with plausible numbers. Reviewers catch those, and one fabricated figure ends the application. Grantwright is instructed to write qualitatively when the evidence is thin, and to say so.

    Request-type framing is applied for you

    A general operating request argues differently from a capital request. That judgment is built in rather than something you have to know to prompt for.

    Your details carry across drafts

    Enter your mission and program once. Every later application to every other funder reuses it.

    Questions

    Common questions

    How long does a grant application draft take to write?

    By hand, a first draft usually takes a small nonprofit six to twelve hours across several sittings. Grantwright produces a complete 700 to 900 word narrative in about a minute, which you then edit against the funder's word limits and format.

    What does the free preview include?

    A real draft written from your own intake and the funder's guidelines, cut off partway through. It shows the tone and the argument before you spend anything. No account required.

    What if my nonprofit has no outcomes data?

    Most don't. It works with what you have — intake counts, staff observations, past program numbers — and writes about them honestly instead of inventing statistics that fall apart under review.

    How much does a freelance grant writer cost?

    Commonly between $750 and $3,000 per application, or $50 to $150 an hour. That's out of reach on a thin administrative budget, which is the gap this fills.

    Does using AI to write a grant application disqualify you?

    Very few funders prohibit it and most say nothing either way. What disqualifies applications is inaccuracy, missed requirements, and generic language that ignores the funder's stated priorities. Read every draft carefully and verify each factual claim before submitting.

    Do you guarantee funding?

    No one honestly can. What you get is a draft matched to that funder's language and priorities, fast.
